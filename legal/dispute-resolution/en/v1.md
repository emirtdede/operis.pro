# Dispute Resolution and Mediation Terms

**Last Updated:** 2026-09-17  
**Version:** v1.0  
**Statutory Basis:** Turkish Mediation Law No. 6325, Law No. 7155 (Commercial Mediation as Condition Precedent), Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100, Turkish Commercial Code No. 6102

---

## 1. Platform Exclusion as Party, Arbitrator, or Judicial Authority

1. **Non-Escrow Direct Model:** The Operis Platform is an independent technological matching network that operates without holding escrow accounts, processing user funds, deducting transactional cuts (0% Commission), or verifying delivery sign-offs.
2. **Strict Disavowal of Arbitral Status:** In disputes concerning technical competencies, source code quality, delivery delays, defective performance, contractual breaches, or unpaid fees between Employer and Contractor, Operis **STRICTLY DOES NOT ACT as a court, arbitrator, arbitral tribunal, expert witness, or formal mediator**.
3. **Direct Counterparty Recourse:** Users are obligated to direct all legal, penal, administrative, and financial claims exclusively and directly against the counterparty to their engagement.

## 2. Multi-Tier Dispute Resolution Protocol

In the event of a dispute arising from a bilateral engagement, milestone execution, or deliverable acceptance, counterparties agree to observe the following escalation tiers:

### Tier 1: Direct Bilateral Negotiation (14-Day Period)
The parties must first endeavor to resolve their dispute through direct, good-faith negotiations grounded in principles of objective good faith under Article 2 of the Turkish Civil Code, relying upon their bilateral contract draft, agreed-upon scope specifications, and written project records.

### Tier 2: Mediation Pursuant to Law No. 6325 (Statutory & Voluntary)
If direct negotiations do not reach resolution within 14 days:
- For commercial disputes, initiating formal mediation is a **statutory condition precedent to litigation** pursuant to Article 5/A of the Turkish Commercial Code.
- The parties undertake to resolve their dispute before an accredited, neutral mediator registered with the Ministry of Justice Mediation Department or recognized international commercial mediation bodies.
- A settlement agreement formalized in mediation possesses the **enforceable legal status of a final court decree** pursuant to Article 38 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law.

### Tier 3: Judicial Litigation and Competent Courts
If mediation terminates without settlement, counterparties may submit their dispute to the competent courts designated in their private bilateral service agreement or pursuant to statutory civil venue rules.

## 3. Platform Lawsuit Immunity and Covenant Not to Sue

1. **Covenant Not to Sue Operis or Vellium:** Users covenant, acknowledge, and agree never to name Vellium or Operis as a defendant, debtor, co-defendant, or liable third party in any civil lawsuit, commercial arbitration, mediation petition, or debt enforcement proceeding arising from counterparty default. Operis is a proprietary product of Vellium.
2. **Dismissal for Lack of Standing & Indemnification:** If Operis or Vellium is improperly joined as a party, immediate dismissal for lack of standing (husumet yoklugu) will be sought, and all legal fees, court costs, and reputational damages incurred will be indemnified by the party initiating the improper claim.
3. **Judicial Subpoena Compliance:** Upon receipt of valid court orders or official subpoenas issued by authorized regulatory or prosecutorial bodies, timestamped telecommunication traffic logs and cryptographic match records maintained under Law No. 5651 will be disclosed strictly to official authorities.
